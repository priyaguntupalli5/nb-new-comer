## Dress for the cold

- **Dress in layers**, with an outer layer that blocks the wind.
- Wear **warm socks, gloves, a hat and a scarf**. Put the scarf over your nose.
- If you get wet, **change into dry clothes** as soon as you can. You lose heat faster when you are wet.
- When the wind chill is colder than **-27**, the risk of frostbite rises quickly. Environment Canada issues wind chill alerts: check [weather alerts](https://weather.gc.ca/warnings/index_e.html) before you go out.
- Severe frostbite or hypothermia needs care right away. Call **911**.

Where to buy: clothing stores and outdoor stores sell winter coats, boots and gloves, and second-hand (thrift) stores often have them for less. Buy warm, waterproof boots with good grip before the first snow.

## Keep your home warm and safe

**Carbon monoxide (CO)** is a gas you cannot see or smell. The risk is highest in fall and winter, and only a CO alarm can detect it.

- Install at least one **CO alarm with battery backup**, in the hallway outside bedrooms. Look for a Canadian certification mark on the alarm and the box.
- Test it regularly, and replace the batteries and the alarm as the maker recommends.
- A smoke alarm does **not** detect carbon monoxide. You need both.

**If your CO alarm sounds:**

1. Leave your home right away and get to fresh air.
2. Do not look for the source.
3. Once outside, call 911 or your fire department.
4. Go back in only after a professional has fixed the problem.

Signs of CO poisoning include tiredness, headaches, flu-like feelings and shortness of breath, and at higher levels dizziness, chest pain and trouble thinking.

**Prevent it:**

- After every snowstorm, check that outside vents for the furnace, dryer and fireplace are not blocked by snow or ice.
- Never use a generator, barbecue or camping stove indoors. Keep a generator at least 6 metres (20 feet) from the building.
- Never leave a car running in a garage, even with the door open.

**Frozen pipes:** during a storm, let a trickle of water run from a tap so the pipes do not freeze.

**Help with heating costs:** if you receive social assistance in New Brunswick, ask Social Development about the **Fuel Supplement** (November to April) and the **Emergency Fuel Benefit** (up to $550, once a year).

## Get ready for storms and power outages

Winter storms can last for days and often cause power outages. Be ready to look after yourself for at least **72 hours**:

- Water: about 4 litres per person, per day
- Food that does not need cooking, a flashlight, a radio and extra batteries
- Your medications
- A backup way to stay warm, if you can, such as a fireplace, wood stove or space heater

Replace food, water, batteries and medications at least once a year. During a storm, stay home and do not drive unless you must.

## Winter driving

- **Winter tires:** New Brunswick recommends four winter tires instead of all-season tires. Studded tires are allowed from **October 15 to May 1**.
- Check your tire pressure often, because it drops in the cold.
- Clear snow and ice from **every** window and both side mirrors, not just a small patch.
- Drive slower and leave more space. Watch for black ice, especially on bridges and in shaded spots.
- Keep your gas tank at least half full.
- Stay at least **30 metres** behind snowplows.
- Pushing snow onto a road is against the law, with fines up to $2,500.

**Car emergency kit:** blanket, candle and matches, snacks, drinks (never alcohol), flares, phone, small shovel, flashlight, ice scraper, tow rope, bag of sand and jumper cables. If you get stuck in a storm, stay in your car and keep the tailpipe clear of snow.

**Road conditions:** call **511** in New Brunswick (1-800-561-4063 from outside the province) or check [511.gnb.ca](https://511.gnb.ca/).

## Winter parking rules by city

Cities ban overnight street parking in winter so plows can clear the streets.

| | [Fredericton](https://www.fredericton.ca/resident-services/parking/overnight-parking) | [Moncton](https://www.moncton.ca/en/my-services-snow-clearing-operations/winter-parking-restrictions) | [Saint John](https://saintjohn.ca/en/streets-and-sidewalks/snow-removal) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **When** | Every night from October 1 to May 31, snow or no snow | December 1 to April 15, only on nights a ban is announced | Only when a ban is declared after a storm |
| **Hours** | Midnight to 7 a.m. | Midnight to 7 a.m. | 11 p.m. to 7 a.m. on the South-Central Peninsula; midnight to 7 a.m. in the North, East and West |
| **How you find out** | No notice: the ban is always on | Announced by 4 p.m. on [Moncton Alerts](https://www.monctonalerts.ca/), with text and email alerts | City website, local media and the information line 506-658-4040, by 3:45 p.m. |
| **If you leave your car on the street** | $50 fine, and your car is towed at your cost | Ticket, and your car may be towed | Ticket, and your car may be towed at your cost |
| **Car towed or questions?** | Fredericton Police, 506-460-2300 | Towed car: RCMP, 506-857-2400. Questions: city, 506-853-3333 | City customer service, 506-658-4455 |

**Snow from your driveway:** do not push it onto the street or sidewalk. In Saint John, the city also asks residents to clear snow around the storm drains and fire hydrants near their home. Salting and clearing your walkway helps prevent falls.
