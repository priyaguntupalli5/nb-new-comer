## Prepaid or monthly?

There are two kinds of phone plans in Canada:

- **Prepaid:** you pay before you use the service, usually every month. There is no contract, and budget prepaid brands often have no credit check. You can stop at any time.
- **Monthly (postpaid):** you pay after each month. Monthly plans often include more data, and you can pay for a new phone in instalments over a contract. The carrier may check your credit, and without a Canadian credit history you may be asked for a deposit.

**Which suits you:**

- **Short stay, or just arrived:** start with **prepaid**. It is quick, needs no Canadian credit history, and you are not tied to a contract.
- **Staying longer:** a **monthly** plan can give you more data for your money once you have a bank account and some credit history.
- **Bringing your own phone** is usually the cheapest way to start. Make sure it works on Canadian networks first.

**About these prices:**

- Most prices already include a discount for paying automatically (auto-pay or pre-authorized payment), so you pay more without it. Koodo and TELUS prices include $10 a month in discounts, including auto-pay.
- **Bundles:** Rogers is $55 for 60 GB and $60 for 100 GB if you also have Rogers internet or TV. Bell is $60 for 100 GB with Bell internet. TELUS takes $15 off your first line if you have TELUS home services.
- **Province:** Koodo, TELUS and Lucky Mobile showed New Brunswick prices. Bell, Chatr and Virgin Plus showed Ontario prices when we checked. Rogers, Fido and Public Mobile do not ask for a province on their plan pages.
- **SIM card:** new activations only, and a SIM card may cost extra: $10 at Lucky Mobile, $15 at Koodo and TELUS. With an eSIM phone you can often activate in minutes without one.
- **Freedom Mobile** is not in the table: its website lists service in Alberta, British Columbia, Manitoba and Ontario.
- **Bell** no longer sells prepaid plans and sends prepaid customers to Lucky Mobile.

Rogers newcomers: Rogers says to bring your passport with your work or study permit, PR card or Confirmation of Permanent Residence, and offers help in over 100 languages in its stores.

Before you sign, check that the carrier has good coverage where you live, work and study. Coverage outside the cities can be very different between carriers.

Prices you see online often depend on conditions, such as paying by pre-authorized payment (auto-pay), buying a phone on a 24-month term, or also having internet or TV with the same company. Read the small print.

## What to bring

- Photo ID, such as your passport
- A way to pay, such as a debit or credit card
- Your phone, if you want to keep using it

You do **not** have to give your SIN to get phone service. A phone company cannot refuse you service because you will not give it. See [SIN and ID](/essentials/sin-id).

## Your rights under the Wireless Code

The CRTC's Wireless Code protects you when you have a phone plan:

- **Trial period:** you can cancel a new contract and return the phone within **15 days** without penalty, if you used no more than half your monthly allowance. People with disabilities get **30 days** and can use up to their full allowance.
- **Cancelling:** you can cancel after **2 years** with no cancellation fee, even if you agreed to a longer term.
- **Data overage:** your carrier must stop extra data charges at **$50 a month**, unless you agree to pay more.
- **Unlocked phones:** phones sold in Canada must be unlocked, and unlocking is free.

## If something goes wrong

1. Contact your phone company first and give it a chance to fix the problem.
2. Still not solved? Complain to the **Commission for Complaints for Telecom-television Services (CCTS)**. It is free, and most phone companies must take part.
