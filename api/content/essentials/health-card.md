## Who qualifies

Medicare is New Brunswick's public health insurance. Whether you qualify depends on your status:

- **Permanent residents** qualify once New Brunswick is their permanent and main home.
- **International students** qualify if they study full time for the full school year with a valid permit. Part-time and exchange students do not qualify.
- **Work permit holders:** the Department of Health has said your permit must be valid for 12 months or more. Call 1-888-762-8600 to check before you apply.
- **Visitors and tourists** do not qualify. Buy private travel health insurance instead.

What you need to bring depends on your situation. The Medicare step in [your checklist](/checklist) lists the documents for each category.

## How to apply

1. Wait until you have moved to New Brunswick. You cannot apply before you arrive.
2. Apply online with the [Medicare application](https://myhealth.gnb.ca/application), or fill out the paper form and send it by mail or email, or take it to a Service New Brunswick office.
3. Apply only once. Do not send a paper form if you already applied online.

Processing takes 10 to 12 weeks. You first get a letter with the date your coverage starts, then your card. No letter after 14 weeks? Call Service New Brunswick at 1-888-762-8600.

Until your coverage starts, make sure you have private health insurance. Students can often use a school health plan.

## What Medicare covers

Medicare pays for:

- most medically needed care from a doctor, in a doctor's office or in a hospital
- hospital care. New Brunswick hospitals do not bill residents for insured hospital services, including physiotherapy and other therapies you get as part of hospital care.

Medicare does **not** pay for:

- prescription drugs (see the Drug Plan below)
- ambulance trips
- eye exams for glasses, and glasses or contact lenses
- dental care, even in a hospital, and dentures
- physiotherapy, chiropractors and optometrists outside a hospital
- acupuncture
- cosmetic surgery
- a full yearly check-up

If a doctor charges more than Medicare pays, they must tell you before they treat you, and you must sign a form agreeing to pay.

## Prescription drugs: the NB Drug Plan

If you have no drug insurance, or your insurance does not cover a prescription, you can join the **New Brunswick Drug Plan**.

- You need an active Medicare card first.
- The monthly premium depends on your income. From November 1, 2026, the lowest premium is $7.42 a month per adult. Children under 19 pay no premium.
- You pay 30% of each prescription, up to a maximum amount per prescription.
- File a tax return every year. If you or your spouse do not, you are charged the highest premium.
- To join, send one application form for your family by mail or fax. Questions: 1-800-332-3692.

## Using your card

- Each person gets their own card. Sign it when it arrives.
- Carry it with you, and show it every time you see a doctor or go to a hospital.
- A parent or guardian keeps and signs the card for a child.
- Your Medicare card is not ID, and it is against the law to let someone else use it.
- Tell Medicare when your address, name or immigration status changes, using the Medicare Updates and Changes form. If you were not born in Canada, include a copy of your immigration document. Medicare can end your coverage or change your expiry date at any time.

## Getting care while you wait for a family doctor

- **Register with NB Health Link** at [nbhealthlink.ca](https://nbhealthlink.ca/). It is the provincial program for people who do not have a family doctor or nurse practitioner.
- **Call 811 (Tele-Care)** any time, day or night. It is free, confidential and in English or French, and you can talk to a registered nurse. You do not need a Medicare card to call.
- **Virtual Care NB** offers online appointments for common illnesses, injuries and infections.
- **Walk-in clinics and pharmacies** can help with many everyday health problems.

In an emergency, call 911.

## Outside New Brunswick

- **Elsewhere in Canada:** show your NB Medicare card to the doctor. In Quebec, a doctor may bill you. Send that bill to NB Medicare, which pays you back at the Quebec rate, so you may get back less than you paid.
- **Hospitals in other provinces:** Medicare pays the standard rate, but some services may be billed to you. Travel and hotel costs are not covered.
- **Outside Canada:** read Medicare's out-of-country page before you travel, and consider travel health insurance.
