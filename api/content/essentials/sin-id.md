## What a SIN is

Your Social Insurance Number (SIN) is a 9-digit number. You need it to work in Canada and to get government programs and benefits. It is private, and only you may use it.

- **Study or work permit:** your SIN starts with 9 and expires the same day as your permit.
- **Permanent residents and citizens:** your SIN does not expire.

Applying is free. To see what to bring for your situation, open the SIN step in [your checklist](/checklist).

## Your SIN is not ID

Your SIN confirmation letter is not an identity document. Do not use your SIN as ID.

## Who can ask for your SIN

Only give your SIN when the law requires it. That is:

- **Your employer**, after you are hired and start earning money
- **Your bank**, to report the interest you earn for taxes
- **The government**, for taxes, Employment Insurance, student loans, grants and pensions

You do **not** have to give your SIN to:

- fill out a job application
- rent a home
- sign up for phone, internet or cable
- get a credit card, mortgage or line of credit, or cash a cheque
- rent a car
- apply to a college or university
- fill out a medical history form

You cannot be refused a product or service because you would not give your SIN when the law does not require it.

## Keep your SIN safe

- Do not keep your SIN letter in your wallet or bag. Keep it in a locked place at home, or on a password-protected device.
- Never give your SIN on the phone, unless you made the call and you know the law requires it.
- Be careful with calls, texts or emails that say your SIN will be locked or cancelled. These are scams.

## If you lose your SIN letter

Service Canada no longer gives out plastic SIN cards, and an old card that is damaged still works if you know your number.

To find your number, sign in to **My Service Canada Account**, or look on your tax return, a tax slip such as a T4, or a Record of Employment.

If you need proof of your number, apply for a **Confirmation of SIN**. You apply the same way as for a first SIN, with the same documents. You keep the same number.

## If someone is using your SIN

Warning signs:

- the Canada Revenue Agency says you owe tax on money you did not earn
- your credit report shows checks you did not ask for
- your My Service Canada Account shows jobs you never had

What to do:

1. Report it to your local police. Ask for the case number and the officer's name and phone number.
2. Report it to the Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre: 1-888-495-8501.
3. Contact both credit bureaus, ask for your credit report and ask about a fraud alert (there may be a fee): TransUnion 1-800-663-9980 and Equifax 1-800-465-7166.
4. Tell your bank, and close any accounts you did not open.
5. Report strange mail activity to Canada Post.

You do not need to report it to the SIN program. A new SIN is only given if there is clear proof that your SIN was used for fraud, because having more than one SIN can increase the risk of fraud.

## Which ID to carry

Carry one photo ID issued in New Brunswick:

- your **NB driver's licence**, or
- an **NB photo ID card**, if you do not drive

**NB photo ID card:**

- Any New Brunswick resident can get one, at any age.
- It costs $48. A replacement costs $15.
- Apply at any Service New Brunswick centre. Bring one ID and two proofs of your NB address.
- You get a 30-day temporary paper the same day, but that paper is not proof of ID. The card arrives in the mail in about 14 days.

Your passport, permit and PR card are hard to replace. Bring them when a step asks for them, and keep them somewhere safe the rest of the time.

## Permanent residents: your PR card

Your PR card shows you are a permanent resident. You need it to come back to Canada after you travel.

- **Returning by plane, bus, train or boat:** you need a valid PR card and your passport.
- **Returning by car:** you can use your PR card or other documents.
- **Card expiring?** Renew it before you leave. You can only receive a PR card while you are in Canada.
- **Lost your card while outside Canada?** Apply for a permanent resident travel document (PRTD). It costs $50 and is good for one entry. Apply for a new PR card as soon as you are back.
- **Your first PR card is free** if you send your photo and address within 180 days of becoming a permanent resident.
