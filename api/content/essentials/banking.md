## Your rights when you open an account

You can open a bank account in Canada even if you are not a Canadian citizen. You may need to go to a branch in person. Bring **original** ID, not copies, in one of two ways:

- **Two documents:** one with your name and address, and one with your name and date of birth. A foreign passport counts as one.
- **One document** with your name and date of birth, and a person the bank trusts, such as an existing customer, who confirms who you are.

A bank can only refuse you for specific reasons, such as fraud in the last 7 years or giving false information. If it refuses, it must tell you in writing and explain how to complain.

Your bank may ask for your SIN, because it reports the interest you earn for taxes. See [SIN and ID](/essentials/sin-id).

## Ask for a no-cost account

- **No-cost account ($0 a month):** you qualify in your **first year in Canada**, as a **student**, or if you are **18 or younger**.
- **Low-cost account ($4 or less a month):** anyone can get one.
- Both include at least 18 debit transactions a month, and the bank cannot make you keep a minimum balance.

Ask for these accounts by name. Also ask about other fees: ATM fees, overdraft fees, fees for bounced payments (NSF), and fees for using foreign money.

Extra perks worth asking about: RBC charges no transfer fee on international money transfers, and TD charges no TD transfer fee on TD Global Transfer for up to 12 months. Other banks' fees and exchange rates still apply.

When you compare, check:

- **The monthly fee after the free period ends.** All of these packages go back to a regular fee unless you meet a condition.
- **How long ago you arrived.** Each bank has its own limit.
- **The conditions of any welcome bonus**, such as a monthly direct deposit.
- **Whether you must visit a branch.** Some packages need a visit to open or activate your account.

To compare more banks and credit unions, use the [Account Comparison Tool](https://itools-ioutils.fcac-acfc.gc.ca/ACT-OCC/SearchFilter-eng.aspx) from the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada. Choose **New Brunswick** and **Newcomer to Canada (first year in Canada)**.

## When you can use money from a cheque

- You can use the **first $100** of a cheque right away if you deposit it with a teller, or by the next business day if you deposit it another way.
- The bank can hold the rest for **4 to 8 business days**, depending on the amount and how you deposit it.
- The bank can hold it longer if your account is **less than 90 days old**. Cheques from other countries are often held for 30 days.
- Your bank must give you its cheque hold policy in writing when you open your account.

## Your money is protected

- If your bank fails, the Canada Deposit Insurance Corporation (CDIC) protects eligible deposits at member banks up to **$100,000** per category, such as savings and chequing accounts and GICs. You do not need to apply or pay, and you do not need to file a claim.
- Mutual funds, stocks, bonds, ETFs and cryptocurrency are **not** covered.
- Credit unions are covered by a provincial plan instead.

## Building credit in Canada

Landlords, phone companies and lenders may check your credit history. Canada has two main credit bureaus: **Equifax** and **TransUnion**.

- Your credit report is free online from both. Your credit score is free from Equifax in New Brunswick.
- Checking your own report or score does **not** lower your score.
- **Paying on time** is the most important part of your score.
- Try to use **less than 30%** of your credit limit.
- Some lenders may look at your credit history from another country. Ask the bank, and bring a credit report from home if you have one.
- Be careful with offers of "free credit reports" from unknown sources. Some are scams.

## Sending money home

- Compare the **total cost**: the transfer fee, the exchange rate, and any fee the person receiving it pays.
- "Low" or "no" fees can hide a worse exchange rate.
- Paying for a transfer with a credit card can cost more, because it may count as a cash advance.
- Keep your receipt and transaction number.
