## Driving on your licence from home

How long you can use your licence from home depends on your status:

- **Permanent residents:** get a New Brunswick licence as soon as you move here.
- **Work or study permit valid for more than 6 months:** you can drive on your valid home licence for up to 6 months from the day you land in Canada.
- **Students:** you do not need to exchange your licence while you are in school. Once you start working or become a permanent resident, you must exchange it.

If your licence is not in English or French, carry an official translation, from a translation agency or an international driver's licence.

## Exchange or test?

It depends on the country that issued your licence.

- **No written or road test** if your licence is from: Australia, Austria, Belgium, Denmark, France, Germany, Ireland, Isle of Man, Italy, Japan, the Netherlands, New Zealand, Norway, Portugal, South Korea, Spain, Sweden, Switzerland, Taiwan, Ukraine, or the UK (England, Scotland, Wales). Taiwan and Ukraine have extra conditions: see the official page.
- **Another Canadian province or a US state:** you exchange it for the same class of licence, with no tests.
- **Any other country:** you take the full exam: a vision test, a written knowledge test and a road test.

## What to bring to Service New Brunswick

- your original licence from home
- an official English or French translation, if needed
- your study permit, work permit or PR card
- one piece of ID
- two proofs of your New Brunswick address

**ID that is accepted** (it must show your full legal name and date of birth), for example:

- PR card or Confirmation of Permanent Residence
- a study permit, work permit or visitor record allowing a stay of more than 6 months
- a foreign passport with your immigration documents
- a Canadian passport or birth certificate

A Medicare card, school ID, credit card or regular driver's licence from another province is **not** accepted as ID.

**Proof of address:** two documents from two different organizations, the most recent ones, showing your NB address. For example:

- a residential lease signed by you and your landlord
- a current utility bill in your name (phone, internet, electricity, cable or water). Two bills of the same type do not count as two documents.
- a pay stub, T4 or letter from your employer
- a home, tenant or car insurance policy
- a document from the Canada Revenue Agency or another Government of Canada benefit

**Fees:** up to $100 for the licence, and $25 plus HST for a road test if you need one.

When you exchange, New Brunswick keeps your licence from home for 5 years. You can ask for it back if you are leaving Canada, with at least 14 days' notice.

## If you need to take the tests

1. **Study the free Driver's Handbook.** It is online in nine parts.
2. **Take the knowledge test**, online or at a Service New Brunswick office. Book in-person tests online or at 1-888-762-8600. There is a fee.
3. **Wait at least 4 business days** after passing, then go to a Service New Brunswick office for your vision test.
4. **Book the road test** online or at 1-888-762-8600. Bring paper copies of everything except insurance, which can be digital:
   - a driver who has had a full licence (Class 5) for at least 3 years, to come with you
   - your valid learner's permit
   - a signed, valid vehicle registration
   - proof of insurance for the car
   - your driving school certificate, if you have one
   - proof that you paid for the road test

If you are exchanging your home licence on the day of your road test, arrive at least 45 minutes early.

Did not pass? After the first try, wait one day. After the second, wait one week. After three tries, you need an in-person test at a Driver Examiner office.

## New drivers: the graduated licence

If you are getting a licence for the first time, at any age, you start with a graduated licence and move up in two levels. It takes at least 2 years.

- **Level 1 (learner):** at least 12 months, or 8 months after an approved driving school. A driver with a full licence and at least 3 years' experience must sit beside you. No other passengers. No driving from midnight to 5 a.m.
- **Level 2:** pass the road test to start. At least 12 months. No more than 3 passengers, with only one in the front. If you are under 21, no driving from midnight to 5 a.m. unless an experienced driver is with you, except for work or school.
- **Full licence (Class 5):** no road test, but you must apply for it.
- **Zero alcohol and drugs** at both levels.

Already an experienced driver, but your country is not on the exchange list? The official pages do not say whether you get credit for your experience. Call the Motor Vehicle Branch at 506-453-2410 to ask before you start.

## If you own a car

- New residents must register their vehicle with Service New Brunswick right away.
- You need proof of insurance for that vehicle to register it.
- Bringing a car from outside Canada? You also need a Vehicle Import Form.
- Since April 1, 2026, New Brunswick no longer uses registration stickers.
